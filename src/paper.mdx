## TL;DR

- A rule-based attributor, human observers, and four vision–language models disagree systematically about what caused the same 284 tracker failures.
- Two annotators independently labelled 41% and 44% of clips in our stratified corpus as showing **no visible error** — an observability gap between what a metric counts and what an observer can perceive.
- MOT20's own annotations explain 22 of 282 apparent visible misses; the rest trace to six classes of annotation contamination.
- The best model reaches κ = 0.144, confined entirely to identity changes. Across four models and twenty conditions, recall on missed detections never exceeds 2.8%.

</HighlightedSection>

## Can you spot the failure?

## The observability gap

Two annotators independently labelled 41% and 44% of clips in our stratified corpus as showing no visible error. Those rates are measured over a corpus stratified at roughly 30 clips per condition category; they are not an estimate of how often tracking failures go unobserved in MOT20 at large.

Two mechanisms account for the gap. Duration: where the tracker held a box on the target before the failure, `no_error_visible` declines from 68%/58% at three frames to 4%/7% above one hundred. Entry: of the 90 clips where the tracker held no box before the failure, 70 are entry events, and there the profile does not decline. MOT20 annotates a pedestrian from the instant of entry, at a median height of 29 px against an empirical detection floor of 59–113 px.

## What the annotations explain

282 events are apparent visible misses: the target carries visibility ≥ 0.8 and no tracker box. Twenty-two survive attribution. Visibility encodes occlusion by annotated objects but not by unannotated scene structure, and it does not encode truncation, so a pedestrian 60% outside the frame can carry visibility 1.00.

| Stage | Events |
| :--- | ---: |
| Apparent visible miss (raw) | 282 |
| − within 40 px of unannotated static structure | −12 |
| − truncated by the frame boundary | −205 |
| − sub-3-frame flicker | −37 |
| − localisation drift below IoU 0.5 | −6 |
| Visible miss (final) | **22** |

Six contamination classes account for the rest of the cascade, plus two matching failures the visibility field cannot represent:

- unannotated static structure
- truncation at the frame boundary
- one- and two-frame flicker
- localisation drift below IoU 0.5
- a stationary standee credited against seven ground-truth identities
- crowd-class frames that count a failure without identifying the missed individual

## Can a VLM diagnose it?

Four models saw the same clips. The strongest result is Qwen3.8-27B under supervision-free rendering: κ = 0.144, confined entirely to identity changes. Across four models and twenty conditions, recall on missed detections never exceeds 2.8%.

## Why not?

On 100 single frames drawn before and within false-negative intervals, Qwen3-VL-8B detects the highlight at 93/100. Event-centred sampling that guarantees eight frames inside the failure window leaves the unbroken-track rate at 0.950, against 0.947 under uniform sampling. Forty rendering conditions, including a ground-truth overlay, produce no condition that clears its selection-corrected null.

## What convention knowledge recovers

A verifier who first labelled clips `no_error` completed a second pass knowing MOT20's annotation convention; 47 of 170 were reclassified as misses. The informed pass was necessarily second, so policy knowledge and order are confounded.
